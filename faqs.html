Frequently asked questions

The things people ask us most often. If your question is not here, call the practice — we are happy to talk it through before you commit to anything.

Do I need to see a dentist first?

It depends on what is in your mouth.

If you have no natural teeth and no implants, no — come straight to us. Clinical dental technicians are allowed to provide and maintain full dentures for you directly. That is what "direct access" means.

If you have any natural teeth, or any implants, then yes. We can treat you, but the GDC requires us to work to a written prescription from a dentist — a specific instruction for the work, not just confirmation that you had a check-up recently. There is good reason for it: a partial denture fitted over an untreated problem makes the problem worse.

Repairs and shade-taking are the exceptions — we can do those for anyone, directly. If you need a prescription and do not have a dentist, tell us and we will help you find one.

How long will it take?

Most new dentures take three to five weeks across four or five appointments. Repairs are usually same-day. Immediate dentures are made before your extractions so they are ready on the day.

We will give you your own timetable at the consultation. Rushing this is a false economy — the try-in stage is where a denture stops being adequate and starts being right.

What will it cost?

It depends on the type of denture and the materials. The treatments page gives guide prices for each option.

You will get a written estimate after your free consultation, before any work starts, and we do not add to it afterwards. Ask us about paying in instalments if that would help.

Will they look obviously false?

They should not. Tooth shape, shade, spacing and even slight irregularities are all chosen to suit your face, your age and — if you have them — photographs of your own teeth from before.

You approve the arrangement at the wax try-in, before anything is finished. Bring a partner or a friend to that appointment; a second opinion from someone who knows your face is genuinely useful.

Will I be able to eat and speak normally?

Eventually, yes, but not on day one. Expect a fortnight or so of adjustment. Start with soft food cut small, chew on both sides at once to keep the denture balanced, and build up.

Speech usually settles within a few days. Reading aloud to yourself for ten minutes a day helps more than anything else. If a particular sound is still wrong after a couple of weeks, tell us — it is often fixable with a small adjustment.

My denture rubs. Is that normal?

Some soreness in the first week or two is common, and it is exactly what the review appointment is for. Do not put up with it and do not try to file it down yourself.

One thing that helps us: wear the denture for a few hours before your adjustment appointment, even if it is uncomfortable. The sore spot shows us precisely where to relieve it.

How should I clean them?

Brush them twice a day over a bowl of water or a folded towel — if you drop them onto a hard surface they will break. Use soap and a soft brush, or a proper denture cleaner. Ordinary toothpaste is abrasive and scratches acrylic, and scratches hold stain.

Take them out overnight and store them in cold water so the acrylic does not dry out. Never use hot water or bleach: hot water warps the base and ruins the fit permanently.

My denture has broken. Can I glue it?

Please do not. Superglue and similar adhesives soak into the acrylic and contaminate the surfaces we need to bond, which can turn a straightforward repair into a full remake.

Put every piece in a bag — including the small ones — and bring them in. Most repairs are done the same day.

How long do dentures last?

Five to ten years is typical for the denture itself, but your mouth changes faster than that. Gums and bone reshape continuously after teeth are lost, so a denture that fitted perfectly at first will gradually loosen.

A reline every few years usually restores the fit for a fraction of the cost of a new denture. We will tell you honestly which one you need.
