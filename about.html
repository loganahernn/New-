About the practice

A small, independent clinic where the person who makes your denture is the person who fits it.

What a clinical dental technician does

A clinical dental technician (CDT) is a dental professional registered with the General Dental Council who is trained both to make dentures and to work directly with patients. It is a distinct registration from a dental technician, who works only in the laboratory, and from a dentist.

What that allows depends on your mouth, and the GDC is specific about it:

  • No natural teeth and no implants? You can come to us directly for full dentures and their ongoing maintenance. No referral, no prescription, no dentist in between.
  • Any natural teeth, or any implants? We can still treat you, but the law requires us to work to a prescription from a dentist — that is a specific written instruction, not simply a recent check-up. Denture repairs and shade-taking are the exceptions and can always be done directly.

If you are not sure which applies to you, call and describe your situation. If you need a prescription and do not have a dentist, we will help you find one.

Why on-site matters

Most dental practices post impressions to a laboratory somewhere else. Each round trip adds days, and every instruction has to survive being written down and read by someone who has never met you.

Our laboratory is in the same building as the surgery. If a tooth needs moving a millimetre at the try-in, we can do it and show you the result rather than book you back in a fortnight. If your denture snaps on a Saturday morning, there is a real chance you leave with it repaired.

How we work

  • We tell you what we would recommend, and what we would not.
  • You get a written estimate before any work starts, and we stick to it.
  • Appointments are long enough that nobody is rushing you.
  • Aftercare appointments come as part of a new denture, not as an extra.
